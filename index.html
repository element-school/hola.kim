The Harbour School Marine Science Center

I led the design and construction of a marine science center and wet lab from concept to opening, managing the budget, vendors and construction professionals. The center sits among public housing, so the build also meant working closely with neighbors, the Hong Kong Government and local NGOs.

Then I built the program to fill it: a hands-on K-12 marine science curriculum where every class met in the wet lab, aboard the school's ship, or on Hong Kong's outlying islands. I taught students with a wide range of learning needs, including twice-exceptional learners, and recommended the accommodations that let them do the work.

Awards

The center is part of The Grove, the school's campus, which won a 2020 World Design Award. It still anchors the school's marine program, and in 2023 The Harbour School was named to the Top 10 shortlist for the World's Best School Prize for Environmental Action. Read the news

A national curriculum, written in two languages at once

Design Squad Latinx

GBH's bilingual engineering program asks kids ages 8 to 12 to solve real problems by drawing on what they already know from home and community, an approach called funds of knowledge. Across four modules and ten one-hour sessions, they learn the design process, build with pulleys, invent their own engineering challenge, and present their designs at a community showcase. I led it from first drafts to national release on PBS LearningMedia, and I wrote most of it myself, drafting Spanish and English side by side so neither version reads as a translation.

We piloted at three sites, in Puerto Rico, Massachusetts and California, worked with Fluent Research on the evaluation, and revised from what kids and educators told us. I managed the cross-functional team, our animation studio and the federal grant reporting, built the curriculum on Universal Design for Learning, and created professional development so club leaders could teach it with confidence.

Results

GBH's later study with Fluent Research followed 541 youth in Puerto Rico, Massachusetts and California. After the online training, 94% of club leaders felt confident teaching the design process. Among Latinx participants, the share who wanted to become engineers rose from 68% to 86%.

What I believe

How I teach and lead

Every child should feel safe, valued and seen.

Equity in the classroom means creating an environment where every child has the opportunity to learn. As a person with many intersecting identities, I know how much it matters for students to have a school where they feel safe, valued and seen.

An equitable classroom starts with awareness: of our students' backgrounds, of the privilege and power we hold as educators, and of the social structures around all of us. We don't shy away from reality. I approach every interaction through funds of knowledge. Each student brings valuable skills, knowledge and beliefs to the classroom, and I honor their communities with historical and present-day examples. It is the same idea at the heart of Design Squad Latinx.

Students aren't served by silos.

The arts are a path to self-expression and, through it, self-understanding. That matters most for students from communities that have historically been silenced.

In my classroom, students have used poetry and song lyrics to understand biomes, deepened their understanding of the periodic table with a play, and used yoga and dance poses to explore the phases of the moon.
